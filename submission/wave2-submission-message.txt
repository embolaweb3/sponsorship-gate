In Wave 1 we told you what we had not done. In Wave 2 we did it, and then we audited ourselves and found a privacy leak in our own contract. This is the story of fixing it and proving the fix on a real chain.

Sponsorship Gate is anonymous, rate-limited fee sponsorship for Midnight. A sponsor funds a pool. Members prove membership in zero knowledge and draw a bounded number of sponsored actions per epoch, so nobody, including the sponsor, can link a request to a person or to that person's other requests.

THE BUG WE FOUND IN OUR OWN WORK
Our Wave 1 authorize() checked membership with a ledger query. The compiler accepted it, and our 16 tests passed. But a query's argument is written into the call's public transcript, so every authorize published the caller's commitment. Anyone could group all of a member's requests and tie them back to their registration. That is exactly the linkability the project exists to prevent. Our tests missed it because they assert on ledger state, and a query changes no state.

THE FIX, AND WHY YOU CAN TRUST IT
Membership is now a Merkle proof. Only the root is disclosed. We verified it three independent ways:
1. Transcript-level regression tests that read what the call actually publishes.
2. A control: the identical check run on the preserved Wave 1 contract finds the commitment. If it didn't, our test would prove nothing. It can fail, and on Wave 1 it does.
3. On a real devnet, a byte-level search of the actual submitted transactions: the commitment is in register (our positive control) and absent from both authorize calls.

WHAT WE SHIPPED
- A real two-party flow. A sponsee with no NIGHT, no DUST and no wallet registered and authorized on a real chain. The sponsor inspected each transaction, balanced it with its own DUST, and submitted it. Deploy in block 358, register in 366, authorize in 372 and 382. Sponsee fees paid: 0.
- A sponsor-side inspector, the check Wave 1 only specified. It refuses smuggled contract deploys (exercised against a real ContractDeploy on the devnet), bundled calls, calls to other contracts, owner-only entry points, shielded movement, replayed nullifiers, and optionally pools too small to hide anyone. A sponsor with no DUST fails with Wallet.InsufficientFunds and leaves no trace in the pool.
- New contract guarantees: per-epoch quota slots, and an on-chain budget the sponsor commits to (the contract cannot read DUST), with an increase-only owner topUp.
- A web console that runs the real compiled circuits in your browser. No wallet, no Docker, no server. Run the guided demo, then open the Linkability lab: the same member on the Wave 1 and Wave 2 contracts, with the leaked commitment highlighted on one and absent from the other. Then try the ten-attack lab, the sponsor inspector, an animated Merkle tree that shows what stays private, and a runway calculator using fees measured on the devnet.
- 70 automated checks: 34 contract tests, 24 inspector tests, 12 end-to-end browser checks.

WHY WAVE 1 NEVER DEPLOYED
The node was silently dropping our transactions. It was newer than the ledger our SDK speaks (node 1.0.0 and indexer 4.3.3, against a validated 0.22.3 and 4.0.0). The pinned, working set is now in infra/devnet/standalone.yml with the reason next to it, so the next team does not lose a wave to it.

WHERE WE ACTUALLY ARE
SIMULATED and LOCAL are done and reproducible; the README has a three-level evaluation guide, from a browser click to a real devnet run. LIVE is not: there is no Preprod or mainnet deployment yet. The same script targets Preprod given a funded wallet. A pool's anonymity is only as large as its member count, so a one-member pool hides nothing. The README says that too.
